# The Look Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A stylised, rich, Freelancer-like look: planets with character up close and from afar, alien
players, a nebula sky, a glowing sun, double comet tails, and proper weapons, pickups, pads and rocks, still
smooth on an ordinary laptop.

**Architecture:** The renderer is split into focused modules under `src/client/look/` (sky, sun, planets,
aliens, weapons, props), each built from a shared asset loader that falls back to today's simple shapes until
(or if never) its files arrive. Planet surfaces are shaders over code-made textures, with the night side,
close-up detail and exact sphere shadows computed in the shader. Models are made by Blender scripts kept in
the repo and exported as `.glb`. An automatic quality step keeps the frame rate. Nothing in `src/sim/` or the
server changes.

**Tech Stack:** three@0.160.0 and its addons (`GLTFLoader`) from the import map, GLSL in `ShaderMaterial`s,
Blender 5.2 headless (`/opt/homebrew/bin/blender -b -P script.py -- args`) for models, Node (built-in `zlib`
only) for image generation, Playwright via `npx` for screenshots.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-look-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- Stylised but richer, like Freelancer (the owner's direction); not photoreal. The CLAUDE.md rule "Simple,
  cartoonish look" becomes "Stylised and rich, Freelancer-like; own assets only" (Task 7).
- Own assets only: everything made here by scripts in the repo (Blender Python, Node) or by shaders. Nothing
  downloaded, bought or copied. No npm packages in the page; three's own addons from the import map are fine.
- Looks only: no change to `src/sim/`, `src/net/`, the server, PROTOCOL or any gameplay number. Looks-only
  numbers go in `src/tuning.js` under `LOOK` (like `FEEDBACK` and `SOUND`).
- Performance: a steady 60 fps at 1080p on an M1 MacBook Air class machine; an automatic quality step (fewer
  effects, lower resolution) when the frame rate drops. Page weight of all look assets at most 4 MB.
- The game starts at once with the current simple shapes while assets load; a missing or failed asset never
  breaks the game.
- The drawn alien matches the hit spheres (`PLAYER.hit`): the head sphere is the bubble helmet's outer shape.
- Night sides darker but never invisible: suit trim glows faintly in the player's colour, pickups and pads keep
  a soft glow, name tags still show; a player on a night side is visible from 30 m.
- Planets stay perfect spheres underfoot; detail is painted or lies almost flat.
- Docs and comments brief, explain why, no em dashes. Tests: `node --test 'tests/*.test.mjs'`. Commits end with
  `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`. Nothing pushed or deployed.

## Review Focus

1. Assets that fail to load (offline, 404, a corrupt `.glb`): the game runs with the simple shapes and logs
   one warning, no page error. Pinned in Task 1.
2. The alien model drifting from the hit spheres (a resized helmet, an animation pose that moves the head):
   pinned in Task 4 by a test that reads the `.glb` and checks the helmet and body bounds against `PLAYER.hit`,
   and by a test that every animation pose keeps the head on its sphere.
3. A slow laptop: the quality step must drop effects within a few seconds and not flip back and forth. Pinned
   in Task 1 (pure hysteresis test) and Task 7 (a throttled-CPU check).
4. Night sides: a player standing on a night side must be visible from 30 m. Pinned in Task 3 and Task 4 by
   screenshots from the look check, judged by the reviewer, and by a pixel test in the look check (the player's
   pixels differ from the ground around them by a set contrast).
5. Online: other players' animation is driven by interpolated snapshot state and must not jitter or snap when
   snapshots arrive; your own first-person weapon stays steady with the online correction shift. Pinned in
   Task 4 and Task 5 by pure pose tests over a sequence of interpolated states.

### Task 1: Foundations (modules, asset loader, quality step, look check)

**Files:** Create `src/client/look/assets.js`, `src/client/look/quality.js`, `tools/look_check.cjs`. Move the
existing pieces of `src/client/render.js` into `src/client/look/` modules (`sky.js`, `sun.js`, `planets.js`,
`aliens.js`, `weapons.js`, `props.js`) with no visual change. Modify `src/tuning.js` (`LOOK`). Tests:
`tests/look.test.mjs`.

**Interfaces:**
- `loadAssets(list, { fetchImpl, loaders }) -> { get(name) -> object | null, ready: Promise, failed: string[] }`:
  starts every load at once, never rejects; a failure logs one `console.warn` naming the file and leaves
  `get(name)` null, so the caller keeps its simple shape. Each look module asks for its assets and swaps them in
  when they arrive.
- `createQuality({ levels: 3, down, up, window }) -> { frame(dtSeconds) -> level, level }`: starts at the top
  level (2), drops one level when the average frame time over `window` s exceeds `down` (e.g. 1/50 s), climbs
  only after `up` s of steady fast frames, never flips more than once per `window`. `LOOK.quality = { down, up,
  window }`. The renderer applies the level (pixel ratio, which effects run).
- `tools/look_check.cjs <base-url> <out-dir>`: opens the page with `?debug&view=...` for a fixed list of views
  (each planet from afar and standing on its day side and night side, the sun, the sky, a dummy on a night side
  from 30 m), saves a screenshot per view, measures the average frame time over 5 s in one view, and sums the
  bytes of every file under `assets/` the page fetched. Prints one line per view and `OK` when there are no page
  errors, the frame time is reported, and the asset bytes are at most `LOOK.budget` (4 MB).

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests:** `"a failed asset leaves get() null, warns once, and ready resolves"`,
  `"a loaded asset is returned by get()"`, `"quality drops after slow frames, climbs back only after a steady
  fast stretch, and never flips twice within the window"`.
- [ ] **Step 2: Run** `node --test tests/look.test.mjs`. Expected: FAIL.
- [ ] **Step 3: Implement** the modules and move the renderer pieces (render.js keeps the scene, camera, the
  frame loop and the wiring). The screenshots before and after the move must look the same.
- [ ] **Step 4: Run** the full suite, the page check and `tools/look_check.cjs`. Expected: pass, `OK`, `OK`.
- [ ] **Step 5: Commit** `Look foundations: modules, asset loader, quality step, look check`.

### Task 3: Planets and moons

**Files:** `src/client/look/planets.js`, shaders in that module or `src/client/look/shaders/`, generators
under `tools/art/`, textures under `assets/textures/`, `src/tuning.js` (`LOOK.planets`).

**The owner's planets:** hot = lava world, glowing cracks on the night side; tiny = small jungle world; mid =
ocean world, up close "full of shallow water, maybe one small island" (a low sandbar); heavy = desert or
canyons; giant = gas giant with bands and storms, and its ring; far = ice world (faint aurora at night); moons
grey and cratered, each a little different.

- [ ] **Step 1:** One planet material (a `ShaderMaterial` per body) with: a base colour map made by code per
  planet (equirectangular, sized within the budget); the sun as the one strong light, day side bright, night
  side dark with the night glow where it fits (lava cracks, the island's lights or none, aurora); a close-up
  detail layer computed in the shader (ripples on the shallows, cracks, sand, frost) that fades in within about
  20 m so the ground holds up underfoot; the ring with its own shader.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Exact sphere shadows in the shader: a fragment is shadowed when the line to the sun passes
  through another body (moons in their planet's shadow, the planet in a moon's, the ring in the giant's shadow
  and the giant in the ring's band shadow), with a soft edge. Players and objects get a soft blob shadow under
  them on the ground.
- [ ] **Step 3:** Pure helper tests (`tests/look.test.mjs`): `"sphereShadow: a point behind a sphere from the
  sun is shadowed, one beside it is not, the edge is soft"` (the same maths the shader uses, in JS).
- [ ] **Step 4: Run** the suite and the look check; look at every planet screenshot (afar, day side, night
  side). Expected: `OK`; each planet is recognisable; the night side is dark but a dummy there is visible from
  30 m; frame time within the budget.
- [ ] **Step 5: Commit** `Planets and moons with character, night glow and real shadows`.

### Task 4: Aliens

**Files:** `tools/blender/alien.py`, `assets/models/alien.glb`, `src/client/look/aliens.js`, tests
`tests/look.test.mjs`.

**The owner's design:** a big head, the biggest part of the body, on a small body; big black eyes; a space
suit; each player's skin is their player colour (`COLOURS`). Calls approved: white or light grey suit for
everyone, a clear bubble helmet, gun held in front, name and health bar above the head, dummy dull grey.

- [ ] **Step 1:** `alien.py` builds the model in Blender from code (low-poly, smooth-shaded where it helps) with
  named nodes `head`, `eyes`, `helmet`, `body`, `armL`, `armR`, `legL`, `legR`, `hand` (where the gun goes),
  materials `skin` (tinted per player), `suit`, `trim` (faint emissive, tinted per player), `eye`, `glass`; feet
  at the origin, up +Y, facing -Z; sizes from `PLAYER.hit` passed as arguments so the helmet's outer radius is
  `head.r` centred at `head.at`, the body within the body sphere. Exports `assets/models/alien.glb`.
- [ ] **Step 2:** Code animation, no rig: `alienPose(state, t) -> { legL, legR, armL, armR, lean, headPitch }`
  (radians) from entity state (ground speed, grounded, boosting, falling, view pitch, hook), pure and tested;
  legs swing when walking, the body leans when jumping, falling or boosting, the head and gun follow the aim, the
  hook arm reaches out. The head stays on its hit sphere in every pose.
- [ ] **Step 3: Write the tests first:** `"the exported helmet fits PLAYER.hit.head within 3 %, the body
  within PLAYER.hit.body"` (read the `.glb` JSON chunk and the accessor min and max in node);
  `"alienPose keeps the head centre on its sphere in every pose"`; `"poses over a run of interpolated states
  change smoothly (no jump above a set angle per frame)"`.
- [ ] **Step 4:** Draw every player and the dummy with it (skin and trim per player colour, the dummy grey), the
  tag above the helmet; fall back to today's two spheres until it loads.
- [ ] **Step 5: Run** the suite, the page check, the two-browser check and the look check; look at the dummy
  screenshots (day, night at 30 m). Expected: pass, `OK` each, the alien reads clearly by day and by night.
- [ ] **Step 6: Commit** `Players are aliens`.
